Build a single-page investor landing site for AI Home Sentinel. One route: /. Static content only. No login, no dashboard, no API calls, no Raspberry Pi connection, no database. A “Request a demo” button scrolls to a short form (name, email, note) that shows a confirmation state in the browser and does not pretend to send email unless a real form endpoint already exists. If it does not, use a mailto: link and say so in the form helper text.

Audience: angel and technical investors who will also see a live demo. Goal: they understand what the product does, why it is different, what already runs, and what is not claimed. This is not a consumer checkout page and not a pitch deck with fake metrics.

PRODUCT TRUTH (do not add capabilities):
AI Home Sentinel is a home security camera that runs on a Raspberry Pi in one home (home beta). Pipeline: camera capture, motion, on-device YOLO detection, face recognition of known people, an event ledger, a reasoner that tiers events, then a dashboard. Optional cloud language model (Google Gemini first, Grok as fallback) for chat, a daily recap, and clip summaries. Cloud upload is a permission. Detection and the recording stay local.

What an investor can see working:
- Live camera and a dashboard with Home, Away, and Night modes
- Motion sessions stored in an event ledger
- On-device person/object detection
- Known faces for household members, plus resident profiles with appearance notes (height, build, tattoos), not face-only identity
- Owner enrollment in plain language, for example “I’m Jordan, make me the owner”
- A reasoner that keeps routine events quiet and sends a phone push only for higher-tier moments: unknown face, a repeat unknown person, a person at night (ntfy, with a cooldown so the phone is not spammed)
- Short clips and a local DVR the owner can review
- Text or browser-voice questions such as “What happened last night?”
- Token-protected dashboard, designed to run 24/7 as a home service

Do not claim: revenue, users, waitlist size, market size, partnerships, awards, app stores, multiple customer homes, always-on wake word, phone calls, professional monitoring, guaranteed identity, or that the cloud stores the video. Wake word and telephony are future flags, not the product. Say “home beta” once, plainly.

PAGE STRUCTURE, in this order:

1. Nav. Wordmark “AI Home Sentinel”. Links: Problem, How it works, A night at home, What’s real. Right side: “Request a demo”. Sticky, minimal, dark.

2. Hero. Eyebrow in small mono: HOME BETA. Headline: “A home camera that knows who belongs there.” Subcopy of two sentences: most cameras send motion. This one keeps a ledger of who was seen, decides whether it matters, and answers in plain language. Primary button “Request a demo”. Secondary text button “See how it decides”. No product screenshot required. A right-side specimen card is allowed: a fake phone push reading “Unknown person · front door · 11:42 pm” and under it a quiet line “Jordan arrived · 6:14 pm · no alert”. Label the card “Example, not a live feed”.

3. Problem. Three short columns, no icons-from-a-pack clutter. Use simple words:
- Noise. Motion is not meaning. A pet, a headlight, and a person at the door become the same notification.
- Amnesia. The camera does not know who lives there, so every familiar face can look like an incident.
- Rented footage. The recording and the judgment often live in someone else’s cloud, behind a subscription.
Close the section with one sentence: the useful product is the decision, not another clip.

4. How it works. A horizontal (stacked on mobile) sequence of six steps, each one short label plus one sentence:
- See. The camera runs at home.
- Notice. Motion opens a session. Stillness stays quiet.
- Recognize. On-device detection and face match, plus appearance notes for the household.
- Judge. A reasoner assigns a tier. Routine stays on the ledger. Unknown, repeat, or night person can reach the phone.
- Remember. Clips and DVR stay local. The ledger is what the system can talk about.
- Answer. Ask what happened at a given time, and you are given the option to read/hear footage description, or be provided a short clip with the footage in question.s optional.”

5. A night at home. A short narrative, two beats, set in type — not a stock photo.
Beat A: 11:42 pm, a person at the door the camera does not know. Night mode plus an unknown face is enough to send one push. The owner can open the clip.
Beat B: 6:14 pm, a known resident. The event is stored. The phone stays quiet.
Then a sample exchange in a monospace panel:
Owner: What happened last night?
Sentinel: One unknown person at the front door at 11:42 pm. No one else after that. Jordan arrived earlier, at 6:14 pm.
Caption: “Illustrative answer. Live answers come from the event ledger during a demo.”

6. What you would see in the demo. Six items, one line each: live view, Home/Away/Night, an event with a tier, a known person versus an unknown, the local DVR, and a spoken or typed question. This is an invitation to the real system, not a second product tour.

7. What’s real. Two columns.
Running in the home beta: the pipeline above, phone pushes for tier-2 events, local DVR and clips, household profiles, plain-language enrollment, dashboard chat and browser voice, optional Gemini/Grok recap.
Not claimed: customers, revenue, multi-home service, always-listening wake word, phone-call alerts, professional monitoring, identity guarantees.
Add one honest constraint: it is one home, one Pi, and a founder-operated beta. The point of the page is to make the system legible before the live demo.

8. Close. Headline: “See it decide.” Three sentences: the demo is the product running at home, on the same pipeline as the page described. No slide replaces the camera, the ledger, and a question asked out loud. Button repeats “Request a demo”.

9. Demo form. Fields: name, email, “What do you want to see?”. Submit shows “Request noted on this device” plus the mailto fallback. Footer: product name, “Home beta”, no social icons, no legal fiction. A single privacy line: this page stores nothing unless they use their own email app.
